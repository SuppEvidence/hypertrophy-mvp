# Athlete-response coaching: source-backed principles

Prepared 11 October 2026. This is a curated synthesis for Hypertrophy Tracker, informed by Alberto Nuñez, Dirk Emmerich and 3DMJ. It is not their official combined policy, an endorsement, or a reproduction of their voices. The application should not claim to speak for them.

The implementation adjusts inference-time instructions; it does not retrain model weights. Sources and analysis stay in this document. Requests receive compact principles rather than articles or transcripts.

## Evidence and attribution

First-person authored articles and a publisher-hosted interview excerpt support the extraction below. A coach's opinion is evidence of that coach's position, not proof of a physiological mechanism. Personal experiments and client cases cannot establish causality. Team membership also does not mean every 3DMJ author shares every position.

Alberto/3DMJ coverage is stronger here than Dirk coverage. The accessible Dirk interview excerpt explicitly describes his own circumstances; much of the interview's written version is subscriber-only. Only its public portion was used. Third-party automated transcripts helped locate topics but were excluded as authoritative sources for implementation. No video or audio was independently watched/listened to during this review. Podcast descriptions and chapter titles were not used to infer specific prescriptions. This is a bounded review, not a claim to have extracted every current view of these coaches.

The archive pages display 2025 dates; these may reflect website migration/publication metadata and are not proof that every underlying opinion originated in 2025.

## Decision principles and application

| Principle | Source support | Application to this athlete | What it does not imply |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Individual response outranks a famous lifter's example | S5, S6; S9 as an individual case | Repeated comparable performance, execution reports, recovery, symptoms and phase-aware measurements determine the recommendation | Their splits, personal set counts or failure frequencies become defaults |
| Effort is intentional; technical failure has an execution standard | S1, S6 | Interpret RIR and near-failure work with recorded execution and protocol | Mandatory failure, mandatory distance from failure, or unseen form assessment |
| Selection can improve implementation | S2, S3; S9 as an individual case | Consider suitable available alternatives for recurring local implementation problems | A universal best exercise, compulsory machines, or automatic rotation |
| Set totals are accounting aids | S4, S6 | Keep deterministic effective-set calculations, but interpret response rather than equating a multiplier with measured stimulus | New multiplier values or exact hypertrophy estimates |
| A useful dose is individual | S5, S6 | Retain tolerated productive high volume; consider staged trials when a real limitation and adequate recovery justify them | A general low-volume bias or extra sets merely because recovery is good |
| Context changes what is useful | S3, S5, S8 | Fit established priorities to time, phase and availability; protect useful work | Time constraints justify covert priority underdosing |
| Maintenance is a legitimate strategic option | S3, S7, S8 | Existing T3 phase advice remains available when its evidence conditions hold | Automatic phase changes, a forced cut or claims of a metabolic reset |
| Respond to observations, then reassess | S5; S9 illustrates revision from observed outcomes | Link a proposed test to its actual limitation and assess repeated comparable responses before stacking changes | An uncontrolled before/after result proves causation |

The last row's explicit test-and-reassess format is our implementation synthesis. The public sources do not specify this app's review thresholds, storage schema or exact decision algorithm.

## Broad communication characteristics

The desired app voice is a synthesis, not a literal imitation:

- Direct and practical: state the useful action and why it matters.
- Patient with short-term noise, but willing to recommend a supported change.
- Respectful of an experienced athlete's effort and autonomy.
- Explain tradeoffs without treating a protocol as morally good or bad.
- Show uncertainty when it affects the choice; do not repeat generic disclaimers.
- Offer specific useful feedback and omit empty reassurance.

The authored material supports contextual reasoning and candid explanation. Extending that into uniformly calm, brief prose, avoiding hype/shame and allowing silence is a product choice aligned with the user's expressed preferences; it is not a claim that all three sources always communicate that way. Profanity, catchphrases, personal anecdotes and verbal mannerisms are not copied.

Hypothetical app wording, not quotes from the coaches or claims about actual recent logs:

- "Your next lateral-raise exposures are still holding up at this dose. The hard sets alone are not a reason to reduce it." This is useful only when addressing a real proposed reduction; do not emit it as routine reassurance.
- "Try the compatible cable variation for the irritated slot, keeping the planned work similar. Compare symptoms and execution over the next few exposures before adding sets."
- "Try a small increase in the priority pattern. Keep it if execution and later performance hold up; revise it if the extra work repeatedly compromises subsequent exposures."

## Changes to the current app policy

Much of the prior policy already matched these principles. The patch refines it rather than replacing the coaching architecture:

1. Historical evidence is described as repeated training response, not automatically measured hypertrophy.
2. Technique, exercise or protocol changes can invalidate direct performance comparisons. Missing execution flags do not prove perfect form, and the model must not claim to have observed technique from logs.
3. Failure is neither mandatory nor a reason to reflexively hold back. The athlete's actual tolerance remains decisive.
4. Before adding volume, inspect evidence of exercise-fit, effort or execution limitations; do not assume those limitations exist. Well-executed tolerated work can still need more volume.
5. A proposed change should be a useful targeted test with a brief reassessment criterion, rather than holding by inertia or making speculative optimizations.
6. Higher individually productive volume remains protected. No dose or intensifier reduction occurs merely because a source used less.
7. Shared tone emphasizes practical explanations, autonomy and meaningful feedback.

T2 and both T3 reviews inherit the shared policy. Weekly planning and workout analysis receive only the compact athlete-response block alongside their existing role-specific instructions. Workout analysis still cannot prescribe volume/program/phase changes. The consolidated T1 engine is unchanged byte-for-byte; its narrow instruction already uses execution, history, uncertainty and actionable explanations. All tier boundaries, models, call gates, validators, effective-set calculations and approvals remain unchanged.

The document is not included in model requests. The compact block adds roughly 1,200 characters to weekly planning and workout analysis; the full programming policy includes it once. T1 has no additional input. No live search or extra AI call is introduced. Exact token/cost changes require measured provider usage.

## Live evaluation cases

These are synthetic review cases, not new deterministic rules or claims about the athlete's actual data. Use them to assess live model behavior after the patch.

| Supplied situation | Expected approach |
| --- | --- |
| Higher-volume priority work; repeated stable/improving later exposures; no symptoms | Preserve the productive dose; do not copy a coach's lower-volume example |
| Persistent insufficient response; adequate comparable history, reported execution and recovery | Consider a staged useful trial within existing bounds; explain what would support retaining it |
| Newly stricter ROM or a different machine; lower recorded load | Treat comparability as changed; do not automatically diagnose lost capacity |
| Intensified work feels hard, but later matched exposures remain productive | Separate acute effort from impaired recovery; no precautionary volume reduction |
| Recurring symptoms in one exercise while other same-pattern work remains useful | Consider a compatible local implementation change rather than an unsupported whole-muscle reduction |
| Sparse history or missing technique reports | State relevant uncertainty only; do not invent poor form or pristine execution |
| Cutting with stable performance and falling waist | No phase advice solely for flat strength; no demand for gain-phase progression |
| Sustained waist rise during gaining plus diminished returns across patterns | Existing T3 phase-recommendation conditions apply; no automatic phase change |
| Tight time cap | Preserve priority work with appropriate selection/distribution and eligible tolerated methods; explain unavoidable shortfall |
| No useful action or observation | Return empty optional prose rather than a status report |

Offline validation/regression tests cannot prove that a live model consistently reasons this way. Compare generated advice with the supplied evidence during dev, and use actual outcomes over time to refine policy. The new instructions do not create an automatic causal-learning system or a persistent experiment record beyond the app's existing intervention history.
